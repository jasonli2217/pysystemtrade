Lesson 7 · Costs

Costs: the silent killer, measured

Why this lesson? Costs are the one thing separating a great backtest from a mediocre live result — and the one thing beginners underestimate. This lesson closes the backtest arc: you'll be able to say which rules are too expensive to trade, and why the system dropped ewmac8_32 for EUROSTX back in Lesson 3.

Two ideas: turnover and SR cost

  • Turnover — how many times a year the rule flips your position around. Fast rules churn; churn costs money.
  • SR cost — the cost expressed as a hit to your Sharpe ratio. Because it's in Sharpe units, it's dimensionless — you can compare costs across any instrument or rule.

The Lesson 3 mystery, solved

Remember ewmac8_32 got zero weight for EUROSTX? Here's why:

ewmac8_32 turnover (EUROSTX)   ~23 times / year   # very churny — it's the fast rule
EUROSTX SR cost per trade      ~0.00116           # cheap market, but...

A fast rule × many trades = a cost drag big enough that the system judged ewmac8_32 couldn't pay its way on EUROSTX, and dropped it. The slower EWMACs (16/64, 32/128, 64/256) trade less and survived. This is cost-aware rule selection happening automatically — and now you can see the exact machinery.

Why this matters for going live Live costs are real money leaving your account, not a backtest abstraction. The SR-cost framework is your defence: Carver's guideline is to avoid any rule/instrument combo whose costs exceed roughly a third of its expected gross return. When you add instruments later, checking SR cost first will save you from trading things that only work on paper.

Cheaper is more than convenience

Lower costs let you trade faster signals profitably, hold more instruments, and run higher turnover rules — all of which add diversification. That's why Carver obsesses over costs: they don't just subtract return, they shrink the menu of what you're allowed to trade at all.

See it yourself (3 min)

# _learning_sandbox/course/lessons/trace_costs.py
from systems.provided.futures_chapter15.basesystem import futures_system
s = futures_system()
I = "EUROSTX"

fast_turnover = s.accounts.forecast_turnover(I, "ewmac8_32")
slow_turnover = s.accounts.forecast_turnover(I, "ewmac64_256")
sr_cost       = s.accounts.get_SR_cost_per_trade_for_instrument(I)

print("ewmac8_32  turnover:", round(float(fast_turnover), 2), " (fast, churny)")
print("ewmac64_256 turnover:", round(float(slow_turnover), 2), " (slow, calm)")
print("SR cost per trade   :", round(float(sr_cost), 5))

Run: uv run python _learning_sandbox/course/lessons/trace_costs.py — watch the fast rule churn far more than the slow one.
