Lesson 6 · Accounts

P&L & Sharpe: judging the system

Why this lesson? Everything so far produced positions. Now accounts asks the only question that matters before you risk real money: did it make money, per unit of risk, after costs? Reading these numbers correctly is what stops you from trading a system that only looks good.

P&L comes at several levels

system.accounts can measure P&L at increasing scope. Same machinery, different lens:

1
pandl_for_instrument_forecast(I, rule)

One rule, one instrument. "Is EWMAC any good on EUROSTX?"

2
pandl_for_subsystem(I)

All rules on one instrument, as if it were the whole account.

3
pandl_for_instrument(I)

That instrument's real contribution inside the portfolio.

4
portfolio()

The whole system — the headline number.

Sharpe ratio — the headline

The Sharpe ratio = return ÷ risk (annualised). It lets you compare a calm system and a wild one fairly. Your ch.15 system: 

portfolio Sharpe   0.478      # return per unit of risk, after costs

Carver's rule of thumb: a diversified system around 0.5–1.0 is realistic and good. Anything claiming 3+ should make you suspicious.

The diversification payoff — in real numbers

Here's the most important thing on this page. Look at EUROSTX alone vs the portfolio:

EUROSTX subsystem Sharpe   -0.037   # on its own, basically a loser!
whole portfolio Sharpe     +0.478   # yet the system makes money
Why this matters for going live You do not need every instrument (or rule) to be a winner. EUROSTX has been a dud in this sample, yet the diversified portfolio is solidly positive — the winners and the diversification carry the losers. This is why you must judge the portfolio, never cherry-pick one instrument. Live, resist the urge to yank an instrument just because it's down; that's often exactly the diversification you're being paid for.

Gross vs net — always read net

gross Sharpe   0.498     # before trading costs
net   Sharpe   0.478     # after costs — THIS is your real number
cost drag   ~= 0.02 Sharpe

The gap is costs. A backtest that only shows gross is lying to you. Lesson 7 opens up that cost number.

See it yourself (5 min)

# _learning_sandbox/course/lessons/trace_pnl.py
from systems.provided.futures_chapter15.basesystem import futures_system
s = futures_system()
I = "EUROSTX"

p = s.accounts.portfolio()
print("portfolio Sharpe :", round(float(p.sharpe()), 3))
print("  gross / net    :", round(float(p.gross.sharpe()), 3),
      "/", round(float(p.net.sharpe()), 3))
print("EUROSTX alone    :", round(float(s.accounts.pandl_for_subsystem(I).sharpe()), 3),
      " (loser, yet portfolio wins)")

Run: uv run python _learning_sandbox/course/lessons/trace_pnl.py (the portfolio P&L takes a minute to compute).
