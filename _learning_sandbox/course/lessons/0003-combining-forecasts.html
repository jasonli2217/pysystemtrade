Lesson 3 · Forecasts

Combining forecasts: many rules, one number

Why this lesson? Your system doesn't trade on one rule — it blends several (carry + a family of EWMACs) into a single combined forecast per instrument. This is the last forecast-side step before the system decides how many contracts to hold. Understanding it is what lets you later add or drop rules with confidence.

The idea: a weighted average, then a boost

In Lesson 2 each rule produced its own capped forecast on the shared ±20 scale. Now combForecast blends them:

combined = ( Σ weightᵢ × cappedᵢ ) × FDM

Two ingredients — forecast weights and the FDM.

1 · Forecast weights — how much to trust each rule

Here are EUROSTX's actual weights from your repo:

carry        0.50     # half the decision is carry
ewmac16_64   0.21
ewmac32_128  0.08
ewmac64_256  0.21
             ----
             1.00     # weights sum to 1
Spot what's missing — connects to Lessons 2 & 7 ewmac8_32 — the very rule we dissected in Lesson 2 — is not here. It was dropped (given zero weight) for EUROSTX because it trades too fast and its costs are too high (its turnover is ~23 times a year). The system literally refuses to trade a signal that can't pay for itself. We'll measure exactly that in Lesson 7. So the forecast you studied is real and correct — it just didn't earn a seat at EUROSTX's table.

2 · The FDM — a diversification bonus

If you just averaged the rules, the blended forecast would be too quiet — because the rules partly disagree, their average is smaller in magnitude than any single one. The Forecast Diversification Multiplier scales it back up so the combined forecast again averages ≈ 10. For EUROSTX the FDM is 1.31.

Why FDM is always > 1 Diversification lowers volatility. Averaging imperfectly-correlated forecasts shrinks the result, so we multiply back up. The more your rules disagree (lower correlation), the bigger the FDM — that's the mathematical reward for diversifying. This is the same idea you'll meet again for instruments in Lesson 5 (the IDM).

Putting it together

Your EUROSTX numbers, reconciled:

weighted average of capped forecasts  ≈ 11.3
                              × FDM   × 1.31
                              -------------------
              combined forecast       = 14.849

That 14.849 is the number that flows into position sizing next.

See it yourself (3 min)

# _learning_sandbox/course/lessons/trace_combine.py
from systems.provided.futures_chapter15.basesystem import futures_system
s = futures_system()
I = "EUROSTX"

weights  = s.combForecast.get_forecast_weights(I)
fdm      = s.combForecast.get_forecast_diversification_multiplier(I)
combined = s.combForecast.get_combined_forecast(I)

print("weights:", {k: round(float(v), 2) for k, v in weights.iloc[-1].items()})
print("FDM    :", round(float(fdm.iloc[-1]), 3))
print("combined:", round(float(combined.iloc[-1]), 3))
